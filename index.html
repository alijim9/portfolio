Project 1

Uso de imágenes de sensores remotos para la evaluación del impacto del uso de suelos de zonas agrícolas mediante índices de vegetación en el cantón de Barva, Heredia en el periodo 2018-2023

Abstract
The monitoring of land covers through remote sensing is essential for the agricultural sector. The analysis of land use change and the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) are useful tools to determine the health of the vegetation in a specific area. The objective of this study is to evaluate the rates of change in land cover in Barva, Heredia, Costa Rica, between 2018 and 2023 by using images from remote sensors to estimate the impact on land use. The classification of land use was executed in the established period using a Random Forest (RF) algorithm, as well as an evaluation of the health of the vegetation using the NDVI. The images used were from the Sentinel-2 sensor, which were obtained and preprocessed on the Google Earth Engine (GEE) platform. As a result, five classes of land use were obtained that correspond to infrastructure, forest management and conservation, livestock and pastures, agriculture, and bare land. A decrease was shown in the area dedicated to agriculture with a loss of 5.86 ha in the study period. For the NDVI, five categories were determined which were no vegetation, low vegetation, medium vegetation, dense vegetation, and very dense vegetation. In addition, a loss of 23.19 ha was observed in dense vegetation and 23.67 ha in very dense vegetation. In conclusion, the degradation of the vegetation cover in the study area and the importance of remote sensing studies for the agricultural and environmental sectors were evident. Finally, it is advisable to promote this type of studies at the local governments for a better land use management.
